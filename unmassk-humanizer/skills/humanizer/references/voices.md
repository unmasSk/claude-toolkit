# Voices

The voice decides what replaces the AI patterns in Pass 3 (Add Human Texture).
Passes 1 and 2 run the same regardless — the tells come out no matter what. The
voice controls the *rhythm, tone, and signatures* of what goes back in.

Pick per the order in `SKILL.md` (named target → mirror from samples → ask once →
default clear-thinker). These definitions are language-agnostic; apply the same
rhythm and stance whether the text is English or Spanish.

---

## clear-thinker

A smart person working through an idea in real time. The writing feels like
thinking, not presenting. (The default when the user just wants results.)

- **Rhythm:** short and medium, with the occasional long sentence when an idea needs room. Frequent fragments. "That's the thing." / "Ni de lejos."
- **Paragraphs:** short, often 2–3 sentences, some single-sentence. Ideas build across paragraphs, not inside them.
- **Tone:** confident, not aggressive. States opinions as opinions. Comfortable with "I think" or "I'm not sure" when genuine; zero hedging on what it's sure of.
- **Transitions:** mostly invisible. Logic carries one thought to the next. Occasional "And" / "But" / "So". Never "Furthermore".
- **Signatures:** real (open) questions mid-text; analogies from unexpected domains; steps back to question its own argument; ends without a neat conclusion.
- **Avoids:** jargon, buzzwords, TED-talk cadence, "key takeaway", "the bottom line".

## casual-storyteller

Like telling a friend about something over coffee. Loose, warm, digressive. You can
hear the person.

- **Rhythm:** all over the place, on purpose. Short bursts, then a long rambling sentence held together with commas and "and". Fragments everywhere.
- **Paragraphs:** uneven. Length follows the energy of the thought, not a structure.
- **Tone:** warm, self-deprecating, honest. Admits mistakes. Humor as a funny person talks, not as jokes. Takes the subject seriously, not itself.
- **Transitions:** conversational and varied. "Anyway", "So then", "Oh, and" — never the same twice in a row. Sometimes a hard cut.
- **Signatures:** vivid specific detail ("that windowless room on the 4th floor that always smells like microwave popcorn"); parenthetical asides; occasional direct address; ends on something personal or half-formed.
- **Avoids:** topic sentences, structured arguments, anything rehearsed, "In conclusion".

## sharp-opinionated

Strong takes, zero hedging. A point of view, unafraid to say it. A commentator who's
been around long enough not to care about being diplomatic.

- **Rhythm:** punchy, mostly short. A long sentence builds like a rant. Fragments for emphasis. "Wrong." / "Cada vez."
- **Paragraphs:** short, assertive, one idea stated hard. No padding.
- **Tone:** direct, occasionally blunt, borderline provocative. Says what people think but won't write. Backs takes with specific evidence. Confidence from experience, not ego.
- **Transitions:** minimal; often just starts the next point. Never "However" or "That said".
- **Signatures:** calls out common wisdom as wrong, then explains why; concrete numbers; picks a side; short closing lines that land.
- **Avoids:** hedging, diplomacy for its own sake, false balance, committee prose.

## warm-professional

Business writing that doesn't sound like a press release. Credible and polished but
still a person, not an institution. Client content, company blogs, newsletters.

- **Rhythm:** medium sentences with occasional short ones for emphasis. Smoother than the others; the variation is gentler but real.
- **Paragraphs:** medium, 3–5 sentences, organized but not rigid.
- **Tone:** approachable authority. Knows the subject, doesn't lecture. Uses "we" naturally. Straightforward about problems without alarm.
- **Transitions:** light and natural. "That's why", "What we found". Never more than one formal transition per section.
- **Signatures:** acknowledges complexity without drowning in it; relatable analogies; team perspective without sounding like marketing; ends on a direction, not a summary.
- **Avoids:** corporate jargon ("synergy", "stakeholder alignment", "sinergia"), stiff formality, and also over-casualness — no slang, no "lol", no fragments.

## mirror (match the user's voice)

Not a preset — built from the user's own samples. When they provide writing, analyze
it and build a silent profile on these dimensions. Do not describe the profile to
them; just apply it.

- **Sentence rhythm:** typical lengths. Short? Long? Mixed? How often fragments?
- **Paragraph style:** length, consistency, single-sentence paragraphs?
- **Tone:** formal/casual, funny/serious, opinionated/balanced, warm/detached?
- **Transitions:** explicit connectors or implicit flow? Which connectors recur?
- **Vocabulary level:** plain or sophisticated? Industry terms or everyday words?
- **Punctuation habits:** em dashes, parentheses, ellipses, commas — heavy or sparse?
- **Distinctive patterns:** recurring phrases, sentence openers, structural habits that make it recognizably theirs.

If they also show writing they *dislike*, study what makes it wrong to them — that's
as useful as the positive sample. A mirror profile is the natural input to the
`protect-list.md` seam: the signatures you find here are exactly what the floor must
not strip.
